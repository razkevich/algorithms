Two-Stack Shunting-Yard — Step Through

Visualizes the algorithm for BasicCalculatorII (LC 227). Numbers always push to numStack. On a new operator, flush while rank(top) >= rank(new), then push.

flush rule:  while opStack not empty AND rank(top) >= rank(incoming): pop+apply, push result

Ranks: + − = 1, * / = 2. The >= is what gives left-associativity — equal-rank ops flush before the next is pushed. Swap to > for right-associative ops (e.g. ^).

Why >= (left-assoc) vs > (right-assoc)

>=   2 − 1 − 1 = 0 ✓

At the second −, top is also −. rank(−)=1 >= rank(−)=1 → flush. The 2−1=1 happens before the next − goes on the stack, so the next subtraction is 1−1=0.

>   2 − 1 − 1 = 2 ✗

With strict >, equal-rank ops never flush. Both − stack up; the final drain pops them right-to-left, computing 2 − (1 − 1) = 2 — right-associative, wrong for arithmetic.

Pattern transfer: Same algorithm extends to LC 224 / 772 (parens) by giving ( rank 0 (so nothing flushes past it) and treating ) as "drain until matching (". For exponentiation ^ (right-associative), use > instead of >= for that operator.
